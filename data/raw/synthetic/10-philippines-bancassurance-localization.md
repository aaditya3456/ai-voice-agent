# Synthetic localization for prototype; requires native-speaker and compliance review

This fictional bancassurance pack is separate from HarborSpring lending. It makes no real insurance promise, coverage representation, or regulatory claim.

## Greeting and qualification

- English: “Hello. This is a synthetic prototype conversation about a fictional protection plan. Is now an okay time to talk?”
- Filipino: “Magandang araw po. Prototype na usapan ito tungkol sa fictional na protection plan. Okay lang po ba ang oras?”
- Taglish: “Hi po, quick prototype check lang tungkol sa protection plan—okay ba magtanong ng ilang basic details?”

Ask only whether the customer wants general information, whether a bank referral is welcome, and whether they want a human advisor. Do not collect health information in the prototype.

## Product explanations

- Premium: “Ang premium ay illustrative na amount sa fictional example; hindi ito final quote.”
- Coverage: “Coverage refers to the fictional benefits described in a sample policy; a real policy would need reviewed terms.”
- Beneficiary: “Ang beneficiary ay taong pipiliin sa fictional example na makakatanggap ng benefit if applicable.”
- Rider: “A rider is an optional fictional add-on; hindi ito guaranteed available.”
- Lapse: “Kapag hindi natuloy ang sample payment, the fictional example may describe coverage as lapsed; a human advisor should explain any real-world terms.”

## Objections, escalation, and fallback

For “Mahal ang premium,” acknowledge concern and say the prototype cannot quote or lower a price. For “Ayoko mag-share ng details,” respect the decision and stop collection. For “Gusto ko ng tao,” offer human escalation. For an unsupported question: “Wala akong verified synthetic information para sagutin iyon, pero puwede kitang i-escalate sa human advisor.”

## Localization rather than literal translation

1. The Taglish greeting uses “quick check lang” and “po” to convey a lighter, polite conversational register rather than translating each English word.
2. “Mahal ang premium” is handled as a concern about affordability, not mechanically as “the premium is expensive.”
3. Beneficiary uses a plain explanation (“taong pipiliin”) before the English loanword “benefit,” rather than dense insurance jargon.
